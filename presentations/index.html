  2. 2023Apr 19

    Organized

    Red scares, political repression, and social work: Yesterday, today, and tomorrow (Webinar co-organizer). Social Welfare History Group, Virtual.

    Social work history

    Program

    The American red scares of the twentieth century point to an evolving political landscape rather than isolated bouts of hysteria that exploded onto—and then vanished—from the political scene. Under the guise of "protecting democracy," organized political forces stifled and repressed progressive social policy, political and social activity, and democratic and constitutional rights. Thousands of ordinary people were ostracized, facing material consequences including imprisonment, deportation, loss of employment, and organized violence. As we will discuss in this webinar, red scares did not exempt social workers—nor have we seen the last one. Panelists: Mimi Abramovitz, Silberman SSW, Hunter College CUNY; Laura Curran, Rutgers University School of Social Work; Jessica Toft, University of Minnesota School of Social Work; Kit Ginzky, joint-PhD candidate, University of Chicago Crown Family School of Social Work and Department of History; Jane McPherson, Associate Professor and Director of Global Engagement, University of Georgia School of Social Work.

  3. 2022Oct 18

    Organized

    Doctoral student interested in (“making”) history (Webinar co-organizer and panelist). Social Welfare History Group, Virtual.

    Social work history

    On April 8, 2022, the Social Welfare History Group (SWHG) leadership team hosted “A Dialogue and Call to Action” event attended by 130 social work educators. The 272 registered participants represented 32 states, 3 countries, and several scholarly disciplines. The discussion highlighted the importance of preparing history-informed doctoral students to (1) conduct historical research that will advance and correct the existing historical record, (2) teach history to BSW and MSW students, and (3) advance social work practice that is historically informed. In response to strong requests from Social Welfare and Social Work doctoral students for the opportunity to discuss their interest in historical research with other students and faculty, the SWHG has planned the following first-ever meeting of Doctoral Students Interested In (“Making”) History. We welcome students interested in: Support for an existing historical dissertation research; Developing an initial historical dissertation research topic; Adding historical content to dissertation, class, assignment, publications; Teaching a social work history course; Pursuing historical knowledge foundational to the social work canon. Panelists: Justin S. Harty, Assistant Professor, School of Social Work, Arizona State University; Mimi Abramovitz, Bertha Capen Reynolds Professor of Social Policy Emerita, Silberman School of Social Work, Hunter College, CUNY; Stephen M. Tomczak, Professor of Social Welfare Policy & Community Organization, Department of Social Work, Southern Connecticut State University
